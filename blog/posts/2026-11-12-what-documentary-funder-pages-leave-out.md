Before each issue of Docket Dispatch, we read the funder's own page for every call. On 17 October 2026 we read ten of them. These are seven gaps that would have cost an applicant time, with the page where we found each one and what to do about it.

## 1. The year is missing

Sundance's deadlines page says "Application opens November 17" and gives no year. The Producers Track page has the full dates: 17 November 2026 and 10 February 2027. The NZ Film Commission lists "Deadline 1pm Monday 9th November" with no year on that line, and the heading above it says 2026. IDFA says entries open on "November 15" and does not state the year.

**What to do:** write the full date, with the year, in your calendar. When a page leaves the year out, look for it on the program's own page.

## 2. The time zone changes

The Sundance Documentary Fund page gave the 2026 close as "Jun 15 2026 16:30 (MDT)" and also as "Monday, June 15, at 3:30 p.m. Pacific time". Those are the same moment in two zones. Screen Australia lists "5pm AEDT Thursday 4 February 2027" and "5pm AEST Thursday 8 April 2027", where one date falls in daylight saving time and the other does not. BFI Doc Society gives 1pm for each round and no zone.

**What to do:** convert every deadline to your own zone and write the original zone beside it. When no zone is given, ask the funder.

## 3. Who can apply is somewhere else

The NZ Film Commission page refers to "feature films" and does not say whether documentary features are eligible. The ITVS Open Call page lists an Eligibility tab, and its contents did not appear in the page text we read, so country and residency were not stated there. Screen Australia's deadlines list gives dates, and the rules about who can apply sit on the program page.

**What to do:** find the sentence about who can apply before you note the deadline. If the page is silent on your situation, write to the funder.

## 4. A required step comes first

BFI Doc Society says "You must apply to the standards before submitting your application." The standards are its Diversity Standards, which have their own application.

**What to do:** read for steps that come before the application, and count them in your timeline.

## 5. Two pages from the same funder disagree

IDA's Pare Lorentz Documentary Fund page shows a "Selection Timeline 2025" and no 2026 deadline. IDA's funding page, in an update dated 10 August 2026, says the fund is expected to reopen in late 2026 with a new theme.

**What to do:** go with the newest dated page, and treat "expected" as a reason to watch the funder, not as a date.

## 6. Some calls take no direct applications

The Pare Lorentz page says: "We select the films through nominations from key film organizations throughout the Americas." Nominated projects submit short applications, and the top 20 finalists submit the Core Application. The page does not describe a way to apply directly.

**What to do:** before you spend a weekend on materials, find out whether a call is open, rolling, or nomination-only.

## 7. The price depends on the date

Visions du Réel charges CHF 30 for submissions on or before 2 November 2026 and CHF 40 from 3 November to 15 December. It offers fee waivers for productions from listed countries. Sundance's page for the Feature Film Producers Track lists a $45 application fee, and its documentary track entry does not state a fee.

**What to do:** submit early where early pricing exists, and ask about waivers.

## A five-minute check before you apply

1. Find the year and write the full date.
2. Convert the time zone.
3. Find the sentence about who can apply.
4. Look for a step that has to happen first.
5. Confirm the call is open, rolling, or by nomination.
6. Open the guidelines document and read it once.

How Docket finds, reads and verifies funding calls

What was done so far in this project, where it falls short, and a pipeline that would make the "human-verified" claim true.

What was actually done so far

Everything in the earlier checks came from three tools. None of them is a scraper, and none of it was verified by a person.

StepToolWhat it gaveLimit
Find candidate funders and datesWeb searchOfficial pages plus many aggregator listingsResults mix 2014 pages with 2026 pages and sometimes contradict each other
Read one pagePage fetch, ChromeFull text of the Docket site; the funder pages I openedCannot see pages behind logins or content loaded late by scripts
Recheck weeklyScheduled task (Thursdays)A dated checklist plus an updated funder-links.mdCovers 5 funders only, and a person still has to confirm

Result: the four sample listings on the Docket page could not be matched to an official open call, and many dates for other regions came from aggregators. Those are leads. They are not verified listings.

The pipeline

Seven stages. The first four can be automated. The fifth needs a person, and that is the part subscribers pay for.

1 · REGISTERA master list of funders and their official pages
2 · DISCOVERFind funders not yet on the list
3 · MONITORDetect when an official page changes
4 · EXTRACTPull dates and rules into fixed fields, with a quote
5 · VERIFYA person confirms against the official page
6 · PUBLISHStatus and "last verified" shown on every item
7 · MAINTAINRecheck on a schedule before each deadline

1. Source tiers

Where a fact comes from decides how far it can be trusted. Only the first tier can make a listing "verified".

TierExamplesUse
Tier 1 · officialFunder application page, rules PDF, application portalThe only source for dates, amounts and eligibility
Tier 2 · field bodiesIDA, Doc Society, Film Independent, festival industry pages, national film agenciesGood for finding programs; confirm on Tier 1
Tier 3 · aggregatorsGrant databases and opportunity listing sitesLeads only. Several showed wrong or stale dates in my checks
Tier 4 · hearsaySocial posts, forwarded emails, blogsTip-offs. Never published without a Tier 1 check

2. Discovery

  • Seed list by region. Start from the funders already found: US, Canada, UK, Europe, India/South Asia, Australia/NZ, Middle East, Africa and Latin America. One row each: funder, program, official URL, region, stage (development, production, post, commissioning).
  • Directories. Government grant portals and state or national arts agencies list their own calls. Paid funder databases exist; check their terms before relying on them.
  • Alerts. Search alerts on terms like "documentary open call" and "call for projects", plus funder newsletters sent to one shared inbox.
  • Reader tips. The site already invites tips by email. Treat each as Tier 4 and send it to verification.
  • Festival and market pages. Pitch forums and co-production markets publish their own call dates every year.

3. Monitoring and "scraping"

For this use, a full scraper is not needed. The goal is to know when an official page has changed, then read it.

Change detection

A page-watching tool checks each official URL daily and flags text changes. Open-source and hosted options exist. Cost is low, and it covers most funder pages.

Headless browser

For pages that load dates with scripts, a headless browser renders the page first, then saves the text. Needed for a minority of pages.

Orchestration

A workflow tool (n8n and Make are both connected in this workspace) runs the checks, stores results in a sheet and alerts the person doing verification.

Rules to follow

Read each site's terms and robots file, keep request rates low, and store facts and links, not copied text, so the digest does not republish a funder's copy.

4. Extraction into fixed fields

An AI model can read a changed page and fill the same fields every time. Every field must carry the exact sentence it came from. A field with no supporting sentence stays empty.

funder, program, region, stage
status        open | upcoming | closed | paused | unknown
opens, deadline, timezone      (ISO date + zone, e.g. 2026-12-21 13:00 Europe/London)
amount_min, amount_max, currency
eligibility   (country, career stage, fiscal sponsor, fee)
portal_url, source_url, retrieved_at
evidence      (verbatim quote for status and deadline)

5. Verification

Each listing carries one of these states. Only the first can be called human-verified.

StateMeaningCan be published?
VerifiedOfficial page read by a person in the last 7 days; date, time zone and eligibility match the evidence quoteYes, with "last verified" date
Machine-readExtracted from the official page, not yet confirmed by a personOnly labelled as unconfirmed
UnconfirmedSeen only on an aggregator or in a tipNo
ConflictOfficial page and another source disagreeNo, until resolved. Official page wins
ClosedDeadline passed or page says not acceptingIn a "recently closed" list only

Human checklist, per listing (about 5 minutes)

  • Open the official page, not a saved copy. Is the program still listed?
  • Does the page state the deadline with a time and time zone? Convert it once and record it.
  • Does the stated eligibility (country, residency, fiscal sponsor, fee) match the digest wording?
  • Does the application portal link work, and does it show the same round?
  • Is it a competition, a grant, or an investment with rights attached? Say so in the listing.
  • Record who checked it and when.

Unclear cases: rolling programs, "periodic" calls, rounds announced only by email, and PDFs. Mark these "rolling" or "unconfirmed" and contact the funder, rather than guessing a date.

6. Keeping it fresh

SituationRecheck
Open, deadline more than 30 days awayWeekly automatic check; person reconfirms at 14 days
Open, deadline within 14 daysPerson rechecks at 14, 7 and 2 days
Closed, next window unknownMonthly check; watch for the funder's announcement
Any change flagged by the page watcherSame day, before the next digest

Countdowns on the site should be computed from the stored date on each page load, never typed in. The sample digest showed countdowns about two weeks out of date.

7. Rules for publishing

  • Show "Last verified" and the verifier's initials on every listing.
  • Link to the funder's official page, not to an aggregator.
  • Do not call a listing "human-verified" unless the state is Verified. The sample digest currently says "Verified Oct 2026" on listings that failed this check.
  • Publish a correction policy: a wrong deadline gets fixed within one day and noted in the next issue.
  • Add a short notice that readers should confirm dates with the funder before applying.

Build order

  • Now. Keep the weekly check running. Fix the sample digest labels and the countdown logic. Add one region (the one you will cover first) to the master list.
  • Next. Put the master list in a sheet with the fields above. Add page watching for each official URL.
  • Then. Add extraction with quotes, and a verification queue for the person doing the checks.
  • Before charging. Run two or three full verified issues and keep the evidence log.

The effort is mostly the verification step. As a rough guide, five minutes per listing means a twice-monthly issue of eight verified items takes about an hour of checking, plus rechecks. That is an estimate, not a measurement.

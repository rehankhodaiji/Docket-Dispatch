Free and paid

A simple split: free readers get the monthly issue with a short list. Paid readers get the full list, closing-soon alerts and a calendar they can add to their own. I haven't tested whether readers will pay for this.

3. How an issue gets made and sent

Day counts are before the send day. Claude does the drafting and checking; you decide and send.

10 DAYS BEFOREClaude rereads every official page in the list and flags changes and failed pages
7 DAYSClaude drafts each item with its quoted sentence and who-can-apply line. Items it cannot support are left out
5 DAYSYou check each item against the funder's page. Only checked items stay marked as checked
3 DAYSClaude assembles the email; you read it and send a test to yourself
2 DAYSEvery link is clicked. Mail previews checked on phone and desktop
1 DAYItems closing within 14 days are read again
SEND DAYYou press send. Nothing sends automatically
AFTERFix errors the same day. Review replies, unsubscribes and clicks after a week

Today the pieces exist as files and scheduled checks. No newsletter platform is connected to Claude, so drafts live in the project folder until you paste or import them.

4. Choosing where to send from

General guidance from what I know of these tools. I haven't checked current prices or limits, so confirm them before you choose.

Platform typeGood forWatch for
SubstackFastest start, built-in paid subscriptions, readers can discover youTakes a percentage of paid revenue; less control over design and automation
BeehiivGrowth tools, referral program, paid tiersSome features sit on higher plans
ButtondownPlain, markdown-based, has an interface for automation, so drafts could be created by scriptSmaller audience tools; you bring your own readers
GhostYour own site and list; low revenue cutMore setup, and you manage deliverability settings

What to check before choosing

  • You can export your full reader list at any time. You own the list.
  • It supports paid tiers and a free tier, and a custom sending domain.
  • It has an interface for automation, if you want Claude to prepare drafts.
  • What it charges per reader and per paid transaction.

5. Rules for sending

This is a checklist, not legal advice. Readers will be in several countries, so ask an adviser about your own situation.

AreaWhat to do
PermissionUse double opt-in: a reader confirms by clicking a link. Only email people who signed up. Never add scraped addresses.
UnsubscribeOne-click link in every email, honored promptly (US law gives 10 business days at most)
Sender detailsTrue sender name and subject line. A real postal address in the footer (US rule)
EU, UK, India readersClear consent, a privacy notice saying what you keep and why, and a way to delete data on request. India's data law and Europe's GDPR both apply to their residents
Domain setupSend from your own domain. Set SPF, DKIM and DMARC records so mail is not marked as spam
ContentPlain text version included; no link shorteners; few images; no all-caps subjects
Payments and taxPaid subscriptions may carry sales tax or VAT/GST in some places. Check how your platform handles it
Your landing pageThe signup form now offers two paid plans, but it takes no payment and saves each email only in the visitor's own browser. Add a payment link and a place to store emails, and say on the page how emails are used, before sending anything.

6. Before every send

  • Every item has a funder-page link and a quoted date. Nothing is blank-filled.
  • "Confirmed by hand" says yes only where you did check it.
  • Region and who-can-apply are on every item. Year and time zone appear where the page gives them.
  • All links were clicked within the last day and stay on the funder's domain.
  • The corrections line is filled in.
  • Test email read on phone and desktop. Unsubscribe link works.

Can Docket run as a monthly paid newsletter, and how to research it without mistakes

Short answer: yes as a low-cost side project, not yet as an income. Your hours and your audience size limit it, not tools or cost.

Verdict

Viable as a modest, honest niche newsletter if you cover more than one region or pick one with regular funding rounds. Weak as a US-only product this month: I found almost no US call that is open now. Sustainable for as long as you can give about 8 to 10 hours a month, because cash costs are small. The hard part is reaching enough readers, which is untested.

Costs

Domain, newsletter platform and any automation tool are likely well under $100 a month at the start. Check your current Claude plan limits, since automated runs use them. The real cost is your time.

3. Do you need to scrape, and from where?

Not in the usual sense. Docket does not need to crawl the web. It needs to read a fixed list of about 25 to 60 official funder pages a month. I tested three places today.

Claude's page reader

Worked for 13 of 22 listed sources. It failed on some with connection resets and a 403 block. Good for a monthly check, but not enough alone.

Plain web requests from your PC

I ran a test from your computer: 18 of 21 pages loaded. This included the Doha Film Institute page that Claude's reader could not open. Three failed: Catapult, Chicken & Egg (it redirects to a new address) and the Hubert Bals Fund (403 block).

A real browser (Claude in Chrome)

The fallback for pages that load dates with scripts, block automatic requests, or sit behind a login. Slower, so use it only for the few that need it.

Recommended setup: Claude's scheduled check does the first pass, your PC's checker covers its failures, and the browser handles the rest. Aggregator sites are never read as a source for dates. Before relying on any site, read its terms and robots rules, which I have not done yet. Python is not installed on your PC, so the Python checker I wrote (data\check_sources.py) needs Python first. Scripts are also disabled in your PowerShell, so I ran the PC test inline instead.

6. What a monthly issue would hold right now

From the pages I read, the dated, open or upcoming items are few and spread across countries.

  • 22 Oct: Screen Australia First Nations Documentary Development (Australia)
  • 9 Nov: NZFC production round (New Zealand; documentary eligibility not stated)
  • 9 Nov to 21 Dec: BFI Doc Society Development Fund, Round 2 (UK)
  • 17 Nov: Sundance 2027 Producers Track opens (US; year not stated on the page)
  • Late 2026, no date: IDA Pare Lorentz Fund expected to reopen (US)
  • 4 Feb 2027: Screen Australia Documentary Production

That is about six items, and only two are US. A twice-a-month or monthly US-only issue would often be this thin. Covering several regions, or adding "closing soon" alerts and an always-updated calendar, fills the gap.

Recommendation: run the six-month trial with the UK, Australia/NZ and US together, label every item with who can apply, and have a person confirm each date before it goes out. Do not claim "human-verified" until that is true.

Pages read 16 Oct 2026. Fees, rates and hours are planning estimates. Funder dates change often; confirm every date with the funder.
